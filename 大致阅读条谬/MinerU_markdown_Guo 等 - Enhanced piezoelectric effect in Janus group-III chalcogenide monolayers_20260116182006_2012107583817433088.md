# Enhanced piezoelectric effect in Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers

Yu Guo, Si Zhou, $^{a)}$  Yizhen Bai, and Jijun Zhao

Key Laboratory of Materials Modification by Laser, Ion and Electron Beams, Dalian University of

Technology, Ministry of Education, Dalian 116024, China

(Received 23 January 2017; accepted 8 April 2017; published online 20 April 2017)

Piezoelectricity is a unique material property that converts mechanical energy into electricity or vice versa. Starting from the group-III monochalcogenide monolayers, we design a series of derivative Janus structures for piezoelectric materials, including  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInS}_2$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInSe}_2$ , and  $\mathrm{GaInTe}_2$ . Our first-principles calculations show that these Janus structures are thermodynamically and dynamically stable. They have a bandgap in the range of  $0.89 - 2.03\mathrm{eV}$ , lower than those of the perfect monolayers, and  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{STe}$ , and  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$  monolayers are direct gap semiconductors. They possess piezoelectric coefficients up to  $8.47\mathrm{pm/V}$ , over four times the maximum value obtained in perfect group-III monochalcogenide monolayers. Moreover, the broken mirror symmetry of these Janus structures induces out-of-plane dipolar polarization, yielding additional out-of-plane piezoelectric coefficients of  $0.07 - 0.46\mathrm{pm/V}$ . The enhanced piezoelectric properties enable the development of these novel two-dimensional materials for piezoelectric sensors and nanogenerators. Published by AIP Publishing.

[http://dx.doi.org/10.1063/1.4981877]

Group-III monochalcogenides (MX,  $\mathrm{M} = \mathrm{Ga}$ , In;  $\mathrm{X} = \mathrm{S}$ , Se, Te), a new class of layered materials with honeycomb structures, $^{1-3}$  possess intriguing physical and chemical properties and have attracted increasing attention. Recently, monolayer group-III monochalcogenides have been synthesized by mechanical exfoliation. $^{1,4-8}$  These novel two-dimensional (2D) materials have a bandgap of  $2.20 - 3.98\mathrm{eV}$ , $^{9,10}$  and the gap decreases with increasing number of layers down to  $0.97 - 3.05\mathrm{eV}$  for the bulk systems. $^{5-18}$  Moreover, the group-III monochalcogenides exhibit high carrier mobilities, i.e., 80 and  $215\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$  for bulk GaS and GaSe and  $12700\mathrm{cm}^2\mathrm{V}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$  for monolayer InSe, rendering them promising channel materials for field effect transistors. $^{5,16}$  These nanosheets also hold great promise for photodetectors, $^{7,14,19-21}$  catalysts for lithium batteries, $^{22-25}$  and thermoelectric devices. $^{26}$

On the theoretical aspect, the atomic structures and mechanical and electronic properties of monolayer group-III monochalcogenides have been extensively investigated. First-principles calculations show that their bandgap can be engineered by strain and external electric field $^{8,12,13}$  and realize a semiconductor-to-metal transition. $^{27}$  These monolayers are predicted to be ferromagnetic and half-metal under a broad range of hole-doping. $^{28-30}$  They have proper band edge positions and are candidate photocatalysts for solar water splitting. $^{9}$  These 2D materials also possess excellent thermoelectric properties with a theoretical figure of merit up to 2.43. $^{31}$

Piezoelectric devices, which convert mechanical energy into electricity, have the advantages of large power density and ease of use for sensors and energy harvesting.[32-37] A piezoelectric material must be non-centrosymmetric. Crystals based on the wurtzite structure are well-known piezoelectric

materials, such as  $\alpha$ -quartz (piezoelectric coefficient  $= 2.3\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V}$ ), GaN  $(3.1\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V})$ , AlN  $(5.1\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V})$ , and ZnO  $(9.93\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V})$ . [38-41] Previous studies predict that the group-III monochalcogenide monolayers have normal piezoelectric coefficients up to  $2.30\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V}$ , [42] comparable to the values of monolayer  $\mathrm{MoS}_2$ $(3.37\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V})$  and  $h$ -BN  $(0.60\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V})$ , [43] and thus are promising 2D piezoelectric materials.

To enhance the piezoelectric effect in group-III chalcogenide monolayers, here we design a series of Janus models, including  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInS}_2$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInSe}_2$ , and  $\mathrm{GaInTe}_2$ . These Janus monolayers have broken inversion and mirror symmetry. Our first-principles calculations show that they have large piezoelectric coefficients up to  $8.47\mathrm{pm/V}$ , over four times the values of perfect monolayers (below  $1.98\mathrm{pm/V}$ ), and they exhibit additional out-of-plane piezoelectric coefficients of  $0.07 - 0.46\mathrm{pm/V}$ . These Janus monolayers with enhanced piezoelectric properties represent a new class of 2D materials for nanogenerators, ultra-sensitive mechanical detectors, and consumer-touch sensors.

DFT calculations were performed by the Vienna ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) using a planewave basis set[43] with an energy cutoff of  $450\mathrm{eV}$  (see Fig. S1 (supplementary material) for the convergence test), projector-augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotentials,[44,45] and generalized gradient approximation for the exchange and correlation functionals parameterized by Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE).[46] For the structural optimization, band structure and phonon dispersion calculations, we used the primitive hexagonal unit cells of monolayer group-III chalcogenides and a  $10\times 10\times 1$  uniform  $\mathbf{k}$  point mesh to sample the Brillouin zone. To calculate the piezoelectric coefficients, an orthorhombic supercell consisting of four group-III atoms and four chalcogen atoms was adopted, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a); the Brillouin zone was sampled by  $11\times 18\times 1$ $\Gamma$ -centered Monkhorst-Pack  $\mathbf{k}$  point grid. As displayed in Figs. 1(a) and 1(b), monolayer group-III monochalcogenides have a layered hexagonal structure. To construct the Janus structure of monolayer GaS for instance, the bottom layer S atoms are replaced by Se atoms, yielding  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SSe}$  monolayers (see Fig. 1(c)) or the bottom Ga atoms are substituted by In atoms, forming  $\mathrm{GaInS}_2$  monolayers (see Fig. 1(d)). In such a manner, we generate a total of 9 Janus monolayers, including  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInS}_2$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInSe}_2$ , and  $\mathrm{GaInTe}_2$ . These Janus structures can be categorized into two types:  $\mathrm{M}_2\mathrm{XX}'$  ( $\mathrm{M} = \mathrm{Ga}$ , In; X,  $X' = S$ , Se, Te and X ≠ X') and  $\mathrm{MM}'\mathrm{X}_2$  ( $\mathrm{M}$ ,  $M' = \mathrm{Ga}$ , In and M ≠ M'; X = S, Se, Te), and apparently they have broken inversion symmetry and mirror symmetry.[49,50] For both perfect and Janus group-III monochalcogenide monolayers, the Ga–Ga, In–In, and Ga–In bond lengths are 2.47, 2.82, and 2.65 Å, respectively. The in-plane lattice constant and layer thickness of  $\mathrm{M}_2\mathrm{XX}'$  ( $\mathrm{MM}'\mathrm{X}_2$ ) systems lie between the values of perfect MX and  $\mathrm{MX}'$  ( $\mathrm{M}'\mathrm{X}$ ) monolayers (see Table S1, supplementary material).

To characterize the thermodynamic stability of the Janus structures, we define the formation energy  $\Delta H$  as

$$
\Delta H = \left(E _ {t o t} - n _ {1} \times E _ {1} - n _ {2} \times E _ {2} - n _ {3} \times E _ {3}\right) / n, \tag {1}
$$

where  $E_{\mathrm{tot}}$  is the energy of the Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{M}_2\mathrm{XX}'$ $(\mathrm{MM}'\mathrm{X}_2)$ ;  $E_1$ ,  $E_2$ , and  $E_3$  are the energies of a M, X, and  $\mathbf{X}'$  (M,  $\mathbf{M}'$ , and X) atoms in their solid phases, respectively;  $n_1$ ,  $n_2$ , and  $n_3$  are the numbers of atoms of each element in the supercell, and  $n$  is the total number of atoms. Our Janus models have  $\Delta H$  of  $-0.62$  to  $-0.33\mathrm{eV}$  (see Table I), close to the values of perfect monolayers  $(-0.66$  to  $-0.34\mathrm{eV})$ , revealing that formation of the Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers is energetically favorable. The dynamic stability of these Janus derivatives is confirmed by their phonon dispersions without imaginary bands as shown in Figs. 2(a) and S2 (supplementary material).

The electronic band structures of the perfect and Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers are presented in Figs. 2(b) and S3 (supplementary material). Generally speaking, the Janus derivatives are still semiconductors with a bandgap ranging from 0.89 to  $2.03\mathrm{eV}$ , slightly reduced with respect to those of perfect monolayers (1.32–2.36 eV, see Table I). In particular,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{STe}$ , and  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$  monolayers have a direct gap with the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum located at the  $\Gamma$  point, while the other Janus structures as well as perfect monolayers are all indirect semiconductors.

To calculate the piezoelectric coefficients of the Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers, we adopt the method developed by Duerloo et al.,[43] by evaluating the elastic stiffness coefficients and the change of polarization under various uniaxial strains of the Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers. In particular, the in-plane elastic stiffness coefficients are obtained as

$$
C _ {1 1} = \frac {1}{A _ {0}} \frac {\partial^ {2} U}{\partial \varepsilon_ {1 1} ^ {2}}, \quad C _ {2 2} = \frac {1}{A _ {0}} \frac {\partial^ {2} U}{\partial \varepsilon_ {2 2} ^ {2}}, \quad C _ {1 2} = \frac {1}{A _ {0}} \frac {\partial^ {2} U}{\partial \varepsilon_ {1 1} \partial \varepsilon_ {2 2}}, \tag {2}
$$

where  $U$  is the energy of a Janus system;  $\varepsilon_{11}$  and  $\varepsilon_{22}$  are the strains applied to the two in-plane directions; and  $A_0$  is the area of the unit cell at equilibrium structure. The strains  $\varepsilon_{11}$  and  $\varepsilon_{22}$  are taken from  $-0.006$  to 0.006 with a step of 0.002. Under each strain, the ionic positions of the Janus models are fully relaxed and the obtained  $C_{11}, C_{22}$ , and  $C_{12}$ , referred to as relax-ion stiffness coefficients, are experimentally relevant.[42,43,51] For comparison, we also calculate the coefficients by fixing the atomic positions under a given strain, namely, the clamped-ion coefficients. As shown in Table I, the Janus monolayers have relaxed-ion coefficients  $C_{11}$  and  $C_{12}$  of 46-90 and  $14 - 25\mathrm{N / m}$ , respectively ( $C_{11} = C_{22}$  for the hexagonal lattice), comparable to those of perfect monolayers (55-88 and  $6 - 21\mathrm{N / m}$ ). The clamped-ion method, on the other hand, gives much larger values  $C_{11} = 70 - 112$  and  $C_{12} = 24 - 34\mathrm{N / m}$  (see Table S1, supplementary material). It was shown for 2D materials that the relaxed-ion elastic stiffness coefficients are in better agreement with the experimental values than the clamped-ion ones.[43,52,53] Thus, we expect that the piezoelectric coefficients calculated from the relaxed-ion method would be more reliable.

$$
e _ {i j k} = \frac {\partial \boldsymbol {P} _ {i}}{\partial \varepsilon_ {j k}}, \tag {3}
$$

$$
d _ {i j k} = \frac {\partial \boldsymbol {P} _ {i}}{\partial \sigma_ {j k}}, \tag {4}
$$

where  $P_{i}$  is the polarization vector;  $\varepsilon_{ijk}$  and  $\sigma_{ijk}$  are the strain and stress, respectively;  $i,j$ , and  $k$  indices correspond to the  $x,y$ , and  $z$  directions. It was shown that the number of nonzero and unique piezoelectric coefficients is restricted by the symmetry elements of a crystal. For perfect group-III chalcogenide monolayers with  $D_{3\mathrm{h}}$  point group symmetry, the only unique piezoelectric coefficients are  $e_{11}$  and  $d_{11}$  using the Voigt notation, which characterize the in-plane piezoelectric properties of a 2D material.[42] For the Janus derivatives with  $D_{3}$  point group symmetry, the unique piezoelectric coefficients are  $e_{11}$  and  $d_{11}$ , as well as the out-of-plane coefficients  $e_{31}$  and  $d_{31}$ .[50,55,56] These quantities are related to the elastic stiffness coefficients as

$$
e _ {1 1} = d _ {1 1} \left(C _ {1 1} - C _ {1 2}\right), \tag {5}
$$

$$
e _ {3 1} = d _ {3 1} \left(C _ {1 1} + C _ {1 2}\right). \tag {6}
$$

To obtain  $e_{11}$ , we calculate the polarization vector  $P_{1}$  of the perfect and Janus monolayers under various strains  $\varepsilon_{11}$  in the  $x$  direction of the orthorhombic supercell (see Fig. 1(a));  $\varepsilon_{11}$  is taken from -0.01 to 0.01 with a step of 0.005;  $e_{11}$  is derived as the slope of the linear relation of  $P_{1}$  v.s.  $\varepsilon_{11}$  according to Eq. (4). For  $e_{31}$ , the out-of-plane polarization vector  $P_{3}$  is computed under various strains  $\varepsilon_{11}$ , and  $e_{31}$  is obtained by linear fitting  $P_{3}$  v.s.  $\varepsilon_{11}$ . For each strain value, the atomic positions of the material system are either relaxed or fixed, giving the relaxed- and clamped-ion piezoelectric coefficients, respectively.

More noticeably,  $d_{11}$  attains 8.47 and  $8.33\mathrm{pm} / \mathrm{V}$  for  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SSe}$  and  $\mathrm{GaInS}_2$  monolayers, respectively, over four times the highest value achieved in perfect monolayers (1.98 pm/V for the InSe sheet). Compared to the recently emerging 2D piezoelectric materials such as  $\mathrm{MoS}_2$  and  $h$ -BN with  $d_{11} = 3.73$  and  $0.60\mathrm{pm / V}$ , respectively,[30] the Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers have even larger in-plane piezoelectric coefficients by several folds, and thus, they are suitable for 2D piezoelectric sensors and nanogenerators. Note that the clamped-ion piezoelectric coefficients for the perfect and Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers are all larger than the relaxed-ion values by 1-5 times. According to the previous studies on monolayer  $\mathrm{MoS}_2$ , the predicted piezoelectric coefficients by the relaxed-ion method agree well with the experimental measurement, while the clamped-ion method overestimates the piezoelectric effect.[51,57] Thus, we expect that the relaxed-ion piezoelectric coefficients of our Janus models are experimentally relevant.

Distinct from perfect group-III chalcogenide monolayers, the Janus derivatives with broken mirror symmetry have nonzero out-of-plane piezoelectric coefficients  $e_{31}$  and  $d_{31}$ , i.e., a stress or strain along the in-plane direction would induce the change of polarization perpendicular to the basal plane. The values of  $e_{31}$  and  $\varepsilon_{11}$  still follow the linear relation (see Fig. S5, supplementary material). The Janus monolayers have  $d_{31}$  in the range of  $0.07 - 0.46\mathrm{pm / V}$ , as shown by Table I and Fig. 3(b). Interestingly, the  $\mathrm{MM^{\prime}X_2}$  systems have larger  $d_{31}$ $(0.32 - 0.46\mathrm{pm / V})$  than those of the  $\mathrm{M}_2\mathrm{X}'\mathrm{X}$  monolayers  $(0.07 - 0.25\mathrm{pm / V})$ . The highest  $d_{31}$  value  $(0.46\mathrm{pm / V})$  is achieved in the  $\mathrm{GaInSe_2}$  monolayer, comparable to those of

some advanced piezoelectric materials such as bulk boron nitride $^{58}$  and  $\mathrm{GaN}^{59}$  in the wurtzite phase (0.33 pm/V and 0.96 pm/V, respectively). The appreciable out-of-plane piezoelectric effect would endow these Janus monolayers multiple functions for piezoelectric applications.

In summary, we design group-III chalcogenide monolayers with Janus structures for piezoelectric materials, including  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SSe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInS}_2$ ,  $\mathrm{GaInSe}_2$ , and  $\mathrm{GaInTe}_2$ . Our first-principles calculations show that these Janus structures are energetically and dynamically stable. Their bandgaps are slightly reduced compared to those of perfect monolayers, ranging from 0.89 to  $2.03\mathrm{eV}$ , and  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{STe}$ ,  $\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$ ,  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{STe}$ , and  $\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{SeTe}$  monolayers become direct gap semiconductors. These Janus derivatives have large in-plane piezoelectric coefficients of  $1.91 - 8.47\mathrm{pm/V}$ , significantly enhanced with regard to perfect monolayers due to the larger change of polarization under in-plane strains for the Janus systems. In addition, the broken mirror symmetry of the Janus structures gives rise to out-of-plane piezoelectric coefficients of  $0.07 - 0.46\mathrm{pm/V}$ . As a remark, Janus structures of 2D materials have been synthesized in experiment. For instance, Janus graphene was fabricated by single-sided chlorination with the other face projected by substrate, and then peeling off the substrate and modified the other side by aryl/oxygen-functional groups.[60] Encouraged by these experimental successes, we anticipate that our proposed Janus group-III chalcogenide monolayers, with reasonable energetic and dynamic stability, could also be fabricated by similar routes in near future. The enhanced piezoelectric effect and favorable semiconducting properties of these novel 2D materials would advance the development of ultra-sensitive detectors, nanogenerators, low-power electronics, and nanoscale electromechanical systems.
