ABSTRACT: The realization of multiferroic materials offers the possibility of multifunctional electronic device design. However, the coupling between the multiferroicity and piezoelectricity in Janus materials is rarely reported. In this study, we propose a mechanism for manipulating valley physics by magnetization reversing and ferroelectric switching in multiferroic and piezoelectric material. The ferromagnetic  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer exhibits a large valley polarization up to  $100\mathrm{meV}$ , which can be effectively operated by reversing magnetization. Interestingly, the antiferromagnetic  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  bilayers with AB and BA stacking configurations allow the coexistence of valley polarization and ferroelectricity, supporting the proposed strategy for manipulating valley physics via ferroelectric switching and interlayer sliding. In addition, the

$\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer contains remarkable tunable piezoelectricity regulated by electron correlation  $U$ . This study proposes a feasible idea for regulating valley polarization and a general design idea for multifunctional devices with multiferroic and piezoelectric properties, facilitating the miniaturization and integration of nanodevices.

KEYWORDS: Valley polarization, Ferromagnetic, Antiferromagnetic, Ferroelectricity, Piezoelectricity, Janus materials

Two-dimensional (2D) materials attract widespread attention due to their remarkable and compelling physical and chemical properties, and they hold great potential for the development of next-generation electronic devices. $^{1-7}$  In addition to charge and spin, the valley is a new degree of freedom in 2D materials, which has broad applications in valleytronics such as encoding and storing information. The valley is represented by the local energy extremum of the conduction band (CB) or valence band (VB). $^{8-15}$  Generally, due to the inherent magnetic exchange interaction and spatial inversion symmetry breaking, the valley polarization (VP) is spontaneously introduced in 2D ferromagnetic systems after considering spin-orbit coupling (SOC). $^{16,17}$  Valley polarization is prone to occur in several 2D single-layer materials, such as the magnetic  $\mathrm{VSe}_2$ , $^{16}$ $\mathrm{VSSe}$ , $^{18}$ $\mathrm{AuCl}_3$ , $^{19}$ $\mathrm{CeI}_2$ , $^{20}$ $\mathrm{RuCl}_2$ , $^{21}$ $\mathrm{RuClX}$  ( $\mathrm{X} = \mathrm{F}$ ,  $\mathrm{Br}$ ), $^{22}$ $\mathrm{VSi}_2\mathrm{P}_4$ $^{23,24}$  and  $\mathrm{VGe}_2\mathrm{P}_4$  monolayers. $^{25}$  It is noteworthy that there is very little research on whether the VP generates in 2D multilayer materials. In addition, whether the VP can be effectively modulated by the stacking modes in the multilayer is worthy studying.

Previous studies reported that the stacking configurations of multilayers can affect its physical properties,[26-28] such as the direction of polarization. The sliding ferroelectricity (FE) via interlayer sliding occurs in BN and AlN bilayers, Graphene/BN and SnS/SnSSe heterostructures.[29,30] And the novel FE

properties in a BN bilayer $^{31,32}$  and transition metal dichalcogenides (TMD) heterobilayers $^{33}$  have been demonstrated experimentally. Additionally, stacking orders effectively adjust the electronic state structure, leading to the layer-polarized anomalous Hall effect (LP-AHE), such as the  $\mathrm{MnBi}_2\mathrm{Te}_4^{34}$  and  $\mathrm{VSi}_2\mathrm{P}_4$  bilayers. $^{35}$  Interestingly, stacking order can regulate the intrinsic VP of ferrovalley (FV) materials, favoring the multiferroics behaviors in  $\mathrm{Yl}_2$ ,  $\mathrm{LaI}_2$  and  $\mathrm{GdBr}_2$  bilayers. $^{36}$

Recently, the  $\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{Z}_4$  family with the Z-A-Z-M-Z-A-Z seven-layer atomic structure have been successfully synthesized experimentally. For example, the  $\mathrm{MoSi}_2\mathrm{N}_4$  monolayer with  $\alpha 1$  phase can be constructed by embedding a  $\mathrm{MoN}_2$  layer into two Si-N bilayers. Researchers also found that this family of materials possesses the emerging magnetic, valley and topological characteristics. For example, due to the broken inversion and time-reversal symmetries, the  $\alpha 1$  phase  $\mathrm{VSi}_2\mathrm{P}_4$  monolayer has the ferromagnetic semiconductor

Herein, in this work, we focus on the intriguing physie properties caused by symmetry breaking in the novel  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer with  $\alpha 1$  phase and the effect of stacking orders on the VP of  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  bilayers. The fully optimized  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer contains a P-Si-P-V-P-Ge-P seven-layer atomic structure (in Figure 2a). The  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  Janus structure is a sandwich structure, which can be regarded that the middle  $\mathrm{VP}_2$  layer is sandwiched by top SiP and bottom GeP layers. It is a member of the  $\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{X}_4$  family and has the possibility of being successfully prepared by chemical vapor deposition (CVD) similar to  $\mathrm{MoSi}_2\mathrm{N}_4$  film.37 Mirror symmetry is breaking with respect to the middle  $\mathrm{VP}_2$  layer, thus the symmetry of  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  (No. 156) is lower than that of  $\mathrm{VSi}_2\mathrm{P}_4^{23}$  and  $\mathrm{VGe}_2\mathrm{P}_4^{25}$  (No. 187). A unit cell of the  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer consists of one V, Si, and Ge and four P atoms, forming a honeycomb hexagonal honeycomb structure. The energy difference between in-plane and out-of-plane magnetization is regarded as the magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE), determining the stability of FM order in monolayers. From the Figure S2, the MAE for  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer equals to  $51.54~\mu \mathrm{eV} / \mathrm{f.u.}$  and is isotropic in the  $xy$  plane and highly depends on the magnetization direction in  $xz$  and  $yz$  planes, suggesting that  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  contains the out-of-plane easy axis. From the Figure 2d,  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer is an in-direct semiconductors with band gap of  $0.10\mathrm{eV}$ . The conduction band minimum (CBM) for  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  is located at the high symmetry of  $+\mathrm{K}$  and  $-\mathrm{K}$  points and contributed by the spin-up channel, forming the degenerate valleys. While the valence band maximum (VBM) is located at the high symmetry  $\Gamma$  point, which is contributed by the spin-down channel. It is noteworthy that  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  exhibit the degenerate valleys in valence band (VB) as well due to the local extrema in VB at the  $+\mathrm{K}$  and  $-\mathrm{K}$  point. The  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer is a bipolar ferromagnetic semiconductor. The indirect bandgap semiconductor character of the  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer can hinder the free charge transfer between the VB and CB, endowing the preservation of polarized charge secondary retention and maintenance of spontaneous polarization. The V atom has a valence electron configuration of  $3d^{3}4s^{2}$ . The intriguing VP is introduced when the SOC is included; see Figure 2f,g. In the CB, the valley at  $+\mathrm{K}$  point moves upward, while the valley at  $-\mathrm{K}$  point moves downward, leading to the large valley splitting  $\Delta E = 100\mathrm{meV}$ . While for the VB, the valley splitting at  $+\mathrm{K}$  and  $-\mathrm{K}$  points can be ignored. In contrast, when the magnetization direction of V atoms is reversed, the valley splitting  $\Delta E$  is equal to  $-100\mathrm{meV}$  in CB. In other words, the valley at  $-\mathrm{K}$  point shifts upward, which is higher than that at  $+\mathrm{K}$  point. It is concluded that reversing the magnetization of the V atom in  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  is a useful strategy for switching the VP. The gap value of  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  is about  $17\mathrm{meV}$  with considering the SOC. In the following, we use a simple model[24,25] to describe the SOC term to get the basic physics of VP in  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$ :

$$
\hat {H} _ {\text {S O C}} = \lambda \hat {L} \cdot \hat {S} = \hat {H} _ {\text {S O C}} ^ {0} + \hat {H} _ {\text {S O C}} ^ {1} \tag {1}
$$

where, the orbital angular momentum  $\hat{L}$ , the spin angular momentum  $\hat{S}$ , the interactions between the same spin states  $\hat{H}_{SOC}^{0}$  and the interactions between opposite spin states  $\hat{H}_{SOC}^{1}$ . According to the Figure 2d, both the  $+\mathrm{K}$  and  $-\mathrm{K}$  valleys in the CB and VB are contributed by independent different spin channels, thus the interaction term  $\hat{H}_{SOC}^{1}$  can be neglected here. For the  $\hat{H}_{SOC}^{0}$ , it can be expressed by the  $\theta$  and  $\phi$ :

$$
\hat {H} _ {S O C} ^ {0} = \lambda \hat {S} _ {z} \left(\hat {L} _ {z} \cos \theta + \frac {1}{2} \hat {L} _ {+} e ^ {- i \phi} \sin \theta + \frac {1}{2} \hat {L} _ {-} e ^ {+ i \phi} \sin \theta\right) \tag {2}
$$

The VSiGeP  ${}_{4}$  contain the out-of-plane magnetization  $\left( {\theta  = 0\text{or}\pi }\right)$  ; thus,the  ${\widehat{H}}_{SOC}^{0}$  is simplified as

$$
\hat {H} _ {\text {S O C}} ^ {0} = \lambda \hat {S} _ {z} \hat {L} _ {z} = \alpha \hat {L} _ {z} \tag {3}
$$

where  $\hat{L}_z$  is the orbital angular moment along the  $z$  direction and  $\alpha$  is the coupling strength.

Considering the contribution of orbital and the wave vector symmetry at the  $+\mathrm{K}$  and  $-\mathrm{K}$  valleys, basis functions are chosen as follows:

$$
\left| \varphi_ {c} ^ {\tau} \right\rangle = \sqrt {\frac {1}{2}} \left(| d _ {x ^ {2} - y ^ {2}} \rangle + i \tau | d _ {x y} \rangle \right. \tag {4}
$$

$$
\left| \varphi_ {v} ^ {\tau} \right\rangle = \left| d _ {z ^ {2}} \right\rangle \tag {5}
$$

where the subscripts  $c$ ,  $\nu$ , and  $\tau$  represent CB, VB, and valley index ( $\tau = \pm 1$ ). Then, the energy of  $+\mathrm{K}$  and  $-\mathrm{K}$  valleys in CB and VB can be written as

$$
E _ {c} ^ {\tau} = \left\langle \varphi_ {c} ^ {\tau} \right| \hat {H} _ {S O C} ^ {0} \left| \varphi_ {c} ^ {\tau} \right\rangle \tag {6}
$$

$$
E _ {v} ^ {\tau} = \left\langle \varphi_ {v} ^ {\tau} \right| \hat {H} _ {S O C} ^ {0} \left| \varphi_ {v} ^ {\tau} \right\rangle \tag {7}
$$

Considering the distribution of the  $d$  orbitals of V atoms, the valley splitting in the CB and VB is expressed as

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left| \Delta E ^ {c} \right| = E _ {c} ^ {K} - E _ {c} ^ {- K} = i \left\langle d _ {x ^ {2} - y ^ {2}} \right| \hat {H} _ {\text {S O C}} ^ {0} | d _ {x y} \rangle - i \left\langle d _ {x y} \right| \hat {H} _ {\text {S O C}} ^ {0} | d _ {x ^ {2} - y ^ {2}} \rangle \\ = 4 \alpha \tag {8} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\left| \Delta E ^ {v} \right| = E _ {v} ^ {K} - E _ {v} ^ {- K} = 0 \tag {9}
$$

Here, we have made use of:

$$
\hat {L} _ {z} \left| d _ {x ^ {2} - y ^ {2}} \right\rangle = 2 i \left| d _ {x y} \right\rangle \tag {10}
$$

$$
\hat {L} _ {z} \left| d _ {x y} \right\rangle = - 2 i \left| d _ {x ^ {2} - y ^ {2}} \right\rangle \tag {11}
$$

The  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  bilayer with the AA, AB, BA,  $\mathrm{AA^{\prime}}$ ,  $\mathrm{AB^{\prime}}$  and  $\mathrm{BA^{\prime}}$  stacking configurations are constructed (see Figure S5). The AB and BA structures are both ground states with the lowest total energy, and six stacking structures have the AFM ordering (in Figure S6a). Except the  $\mathrm{AB^{\prime}}$  and  $\mathrm{BA^{\prime}}$ , the other four structures possess the out-of-plane easy axis (in Figure S6b), contributing to get spontaneous VP. Here, we mainly focus on the AB and BA stacking states. For the AB (BA) stacking bilayer (in Figure 3a,b), the Si (V) atom of the upper layer is located directly above the V (Si) atom of the lower layer, the V (P) atom of the upper layer sits directly above the P (V) atom of the lower layer, and the P (Si) of the upper layer is right above the Si (P) of the lower layer, resulting in the out-of-plane electric polarization  $P$  produced in the AB (BA) stacking bilayer. As shown in Figure 3c,d, the positive (negative) discontinuity  $\Delta \Phi = 18.42$  (-18.42) meV between the vacuum levels of upper and lower layers clearly verifies the spontaneous  $P$  pointing upward (downward) in the AB (BA) stacking bilayer. The lattice constant  $a$  of the fully relaxed  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  bilayer with AB and BA stacking configurations is  $3.53\AA$ , and their interlayer distance  $d$  is  $3.19\AA$ . The AB and BA stacking bilayers can realize mutual transformation through interlayer sliding, so we can conclude that the sliding FE exists in  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  bilayer, which is also demonstrated from the general theory for bilayer stacking ferroelectricity.[46] Due to

charge transfer between the upper and lower layers, the AB (BA) state exhibits strong out-of-plane piezoelectric polarization of  $0.87 \times 10^{-10}$ $(-0.87 \times 10^{-10})$  C/m. Based on the berry phase method,[47,48] the ferroelectric polarization for AB (BA) state is  $2.01 \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{C/m}$ , which is comparable to that of BN  $(2.08 \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{C/m})^{29}$  and  $\mathrm{MoSi}_2\mathrm{N}_4$ $(3.36 \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{C/m})^{49}$  bilayers.

There are two ways to switch polarization in  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  bilayer: (i) the AB stacking state transitions to AA stacking with mirror symmetry (in Figure 3e), and then to BA stacking configuration. (ii) the AB stacking state is directly transformed into the BA stacking state through the intermediate paraelectric phase (PE) state. From Figure 3f, it is found that the FE switching energy barrier through the first (i) way is 0.069 eV/f.u., while that through the second (ii) way is only 0.017 eV/f.u. Therefore, the FE switching between AB and BA stacked bilayer  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  tends to be achieved in the (ii) way. # CONCLUSION

In summary, we propose a mechanism for manipulating valley physics by magnetization reversing and ferroelectric switching in multiferroic and piezoelectric materials. The mechanism is demonstrated in the  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer and bilayers. The ferromagnetic  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer exhibits a large VP of 100 meV, which can be effectively operated by reversing magnetization. Interestingly, the coexistence of valley and ferroelectric polarizations is realized in  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  bilayers with antiferromagnetic stacking states. The VP can be reversed by controlling the direction of ferroelectric polarization by interlayer sliding in the AB and BA stacking states. In addition, the  $\mathrm{VSiGeP_4}$  monolayer contains remarkable in-plane and out-of-plane piezoelectric properties, which can be regulated by electron correlation  $U$ . This study proposes a feasible idea for the regulation of valley polarization and a general design idea for multifunctional devices with a multiferroic property and piezoelectric response, supporting the miniaturization and integration of nanodevices.
