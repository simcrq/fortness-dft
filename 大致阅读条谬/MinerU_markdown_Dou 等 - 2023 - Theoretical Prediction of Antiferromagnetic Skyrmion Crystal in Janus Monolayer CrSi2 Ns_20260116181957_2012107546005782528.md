ABSTRACT: An antiferromagnetic skyrmion crystal (AF-SkX), a regular array of antiferromagnetic skyrmions, is a fundamental phenomenon in the field of condensed-matter physics. So far, very few proposals have been made to realize the AF-SkX, and most have been based on three-dimensional (3D) materials. Herein, using first-principles calculations and Monte Carlo simulations, we report the identification of AF-SkX in a two-dimensional lattice of the Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . Arising from the broken inversion symmetry and strong spin-orbit coupling, a large Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is obtained in the Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . This, combined with the geometric frustration of its triangular lattice, gives rise to the skyrmion physics and long-sought AF-SkX in the presence of an external magnetic field. More intriguingly, this system presents

two different antiferromagnetic skyrmion phases, and such a phenomenon is distinct from those reported in 3D systems. Furthermore, by contacting with  $\mathrm{Sc}_2\mathrm{CO}_2$ , the creation and annihilation of AF-SkX in Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  can be achieved through ferroelectricity. These findings greatly enrich the research on antiferromagnetic skyrmions.

KEYWORDS: antiferromagnetic skyrmion crystal, two-dimensional lattice, first-principles, Dzyalohinskii-Moriya interaction, Janus monolayer

Magnetic skyrmions are localized vortex- or anti-vortex-like spin structures characterized by spins wrapping a unit sphere and protected by an integer topological charge. Their topological properties lead to the exceptional inherent stability against transitions into trivial spin structures, making them technologically promising for data-storage applications. On a fundamental level, magnetic skyrmions emphasize the role of topology in condensed-matter physics. In most systems observed, noncoplanarity in the directions of spins at different lattice sites correlates to a ferromagnetic (FM) exchange interaction: namely, FM skyrmions. Nonetheless, FM skyrmions have shortcomings in common with other spintronics: for example, the sensitivity to stray field. In contrast, topological spin structures in antiferromagnetic (AFM) lattices, consisting of compensated FM sublattices, generate no stray field; moreover, they have been demonstrated to host ultrafast spin dynamics and relativistic motion of textures. Therefore, generating AFM skyrmions is crucial for both fundamental research and applications.

Despite the huge interest, AFM skyrmions have been experimentally observed only in several thin-film systems, including  $\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3\text{-Pt}$ ,[37]  $\mathrm{MnSc}_2\mathrm{S}_4$ ,[38] and synthetic antiferromagnets.[39-41] In these few existing systems, isolated AFM skyrmions are usually observed, while the AFM skyrmion crystal (AF-SkX), a regular array of antiferromagnetic skyrmions, is rarely identified.[38] With the recent rise of long-range magnetic ordering in two-dimensional (2D) lattices, atomically thin AFM materials have gained a great deal of attention.[42-45] Physically, triggered by competing exchange interactions, 2D AFM systems can manifest topological spin textures as well.[30-35] For examples, Rosales et al. explored the existence of AF-SkX in a triangular lattice using a frustrated

In this work, we show that an AF-SkX can be realized in a 2D lattice of Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  based on first-principles calculations and Monte Carlo (MC) simulations. Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  is demonstrated to exhibit a large Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) due to the joint effect of the broken inversion symmetry and strong spin-orbit coupling (SOC). This, along with the geometric frustration of its triangular lattice, stabilizes the skyrmion physics and AF-SkX under an external magnetic field. With the introduction of the skyrmion number  $Q$  and total chirality  $\chi_{\mathrm{L}}$  as measurements, the evaluation of AFM topological spin textures in Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  with the magnetic field and temperature is mapped out. Distinct from those reported in 3D systems, it can manifest two different antiferromagnetic skyrmion phases. In addition, through contact with  $\mathrm{Sc}_2\mathrm{CO}_2$ , the AF-SkX in Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  can be created or annihilated by ferroelectric switching. Our works promote further study on AFM skyrmion physics in a 2D lattice.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Figure 1a presents the crystal structure of Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  which is derived from the prototype  $\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{Z}_4$ $(\mathbf{M} =$  early transition metal,  $\mathrm{A} = \mathrm{Si}$  Ge,and  $\mathrm{Z} = \mathrm{N}$  P,or As). Each unit cell contains one Cr, two Si, two N, and two As atoms, which are stacked in the sequence of  $\mathrm{N - Si - N - Cr - }$  As-Si-As. It has a triangular lattice structure with the space group  $P3m1$  . Therefore, the inversion symmetry is broken. The optimized lattice constant of Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  is optimized to be 3.12 Å. The elastic constants of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  are found to be  $C_{11} = C_{22} = 31.6~\mathrm{Nm}^{-1}$ $C_{12} = C_{21}$ $= -26.9~\mathrm{Nm}^{-1}$  ,and  $C_{66} = 31.2~\mathrm{Nm}^{-1}$  ,which satisfy the Born-Huang criteria  $(C_{11}C_{22} - C_{12}^{2} > 0$  and  $C_{66} > 0)$  ,58-60 suggesting its mechanical stability. We also calculate the formation energy of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  , which is defined as  $E_{\mathrm{f}} = E_{\mathrm{tot}} - E_{\mathrm{Cr}} - 2E_{\mathrm{Si}} - 2E_{\mathrm{N}} - 2E_{\mathrm{As}}$  .Here,  $E_{\mathrm{Cr}},E_{\mathrm{Si}},E_{\mathrm{N}},$  and  $E_{\mathrm{As}}$  are the chemical potentials of Cr, Si, N, and As, respectively (taken from Cr bulk, silicene,  $\mathbf{N}_2,$  and arsenene).  $E_{\mathrm{f}}$  is calculated to be  $-11.23\mathrm{eV}$  , indicating that it is thermodynamically favorable.61 In experiments,  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  is expected to be

synthesized by a chemical vapor deposition method, similar to its prototype  $\mathrm{MoSi}_2\mathrm{N}_4$ .<sup>53</sup>

The valence electronic configuration of Cr atom is  $3\mathrm{d}^{5}4\mathrm{s}^{1}$ . By coordinating with three N and three As atoms in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ , each Cr donates three electrons for bonding, resulting in the valence electronic configuration  $3\mathrm{d}^{3}4\mathrm{s}^{0}$ . As shown in Figure 1a, the six neighboring N/As atoms form a distorted-trigonal-prismatic geometry for the Cr atom. In such a coordination environment, the d orbitals of the Cr atom roughly split into three groups, i.e.,  $d_{z^2}$ ,  $e_1(d_{xy},d_{x^2 -y^2})$ , and  $e_2(d_{xz},d_{yz})$ . The three remaining valence electrons of Cr atom half-fill the a  $d_{z^2}$  and  $e_1(d_{xy},d_{x^2 -y^2})$  orbitals, which would give rise to a magnetic moment of  $3\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$ . Our first-principles calculations confirm that the magnetic moment distributed on Cr atom is  $2.92\mu_{\mathrm{B}}$ . Therefore, monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  is a 2D magnetic material.

The existence of magnetic moments cannot guarantee spin ordering, and thus it is necessary to study the preferred spin configuration. Two magnetic couplings are considered: FM coupling and AFM coupling. Based on a  $1 \times 2$  supercell, the AFM coupling spin configuration is  $72.1\mathrm{meV}$  (i.e.,  $36.1\mathrm{meV}/$  fu) in lower energy than the FM coupling configuration, which indicates that the AFM spin configuration is more favorable. The energy difference is similar to those other reported systems, such as  $\mathrm{VS}_2 / \mathrm{VSe}_2^{62}$  and  $\mathrm{CoGa}_2\mathrm{S}_4^{63}$ . The band structure and projected density of states (PDOS) of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  are shown in Figure S3. Obviously, it exhibits a metallic nature. The band structure around the Fermi level is contributed by Cr d and As p orbitals.

To further investigate the magnetic properties, we construct the Heisenberg type spin Hamiltonian model of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  which consists of the nearest sites isotropic Heisenberg exchange interaction, the anisotropic Heisenberg exchange interaction, DMI, the single-ion anisotropy (SIA), and the Zeeman interaction (see Note 2 in the Supporting Information for a detailed discussion or the model). The effective Hamiltonian can be expressed as

$$
\begin{array}{l} H = - J \sum_ {\langle i, j \rangle} \left(\mathbf {S} _ {i} \cdot \mathbf {S} _ {j}\right) - \sum_ {\langle i, j \rangle} d _ {x y} \cdot \left(\mathbf {S} _ {i} \times \mathbf {S} _ {j}\right) - \lambda \sum_ {\langle i, j \rangle} \left(S _ {i} ^ {z} S _ {j} ^ {z}\right) \\ - K \sum_ {i} \left(S _ {i} ^ {z}\right) ^ {2} - h \sum_ {i} S _ {i} ^ {z} \\ \end{array}
$$

The isotropic exchange interaction parameter  $J$  is calculated to be  $-9.013\mathrm{meV}$ , which indicates an AFM neighboring interaction for monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . It should be noted that the magnitude of  $J$  is comparable to those of  $\mathrm{NiI}_2$ ,[64]  $\mathrm{CrX}$  ( $X = P, As, and Sb$ ),[65] and  $\mathrm{FeOOH}$ .[66] As schematically shown in Figure 1b, such an AFM neighboring interaction in a triangular lattice cannot result in an antiparallel arrangement for the magnetic moments of every two atoms at vertices of a unit equilateral triangle. This would lead to geometric frustration in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . Namely, the Cr lattice presents a three-sublattice  $120^{\circ}$  structure,[67] wherein the spins in each sublattice (colored in Figure 1c) are parallelly arranged in parallel.

According to Moriya's rule, the symmetry of triangular lattice enforces that  $D_{ij}$  for the nearest-neighboring Cr atoms is perpendicular to their bond. The in-plane DMI parameter  $d_{xy}$  is calculated to be 1.960 meV (detailed in Note 4 in the Supporting Information). Such a large value can be attributed to the combined effect of the large SOC strength within the As atom and the large electronegativity difference between N and As atoms. This results in  $d_{xy} / J = 0.217$  for monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ , which is located in the typical range for the formation of skyrmions (around 0.1-0.2). The SIA parameter  $K$  is calculated to be -0.459 meV, indicating the in-plane magnetic anisotropy. The anisotropic exchange interaction parameter  $\lambda$  is estimated to be 0.122, which favors out-of-plane magnetic anisotropy. Obviously,  $K$  plays a dominant role, estimating the in-plane magnetic anisotropy for monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . In this regard, monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  holds the possibility for hosting nontrivial topological spin structures.

Based on the first-principles calculations parametrized Hamiltonian, we perform parallel tempering MC simulations to explore the spin configurations in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . As displayed in Figure S5, it exhibits a striped-like spin texture (referred to as the spin spiral (SS) phase). We then investigate its spin structures under different external magnetic fields and temperatures. Here, we introduce the skyrmion number  $Q$  and chirality  $\chi_{\mathrm{L}}$  to detect the spin textures effectively. The skyrmion number  $Q$  and chirality  $\chi_{\mathrm{L}}$  are defined as<sup>32</sup>

$$
Q = \frac {1}{4 \pi} \sum_ {n} q
$$

$$
\chi_ {\mathrm {L}} = \frac {1}{8 \pi} \sum_ {n} \left[ \chi_ {\mathrm {L}, r _ {n} ^ {(1)}} + \chi_ {\mathrm {L}, r _ {n} ^ {(2)}} \right]
$$

with

$$
\tan \frac {q}{2} = A _ {\mathrm {L}, r _ {n} ^ {(1)}} \chi_ {\mathrm {L}, r _ {n} ^ {(1)}} + A _ {\mathrm {L}, r _ {n} ^ {(2)}} \chi_ {\mathrm {L}, r _ {n} ^ {(2)}}
$$

Here,

$A_{\mathrm{L},r_n^{(1,2)}} = 1 / [1 + S_i^{(1,2)}\cdot S_j^{(1,2)} + S_j^{(1,2)}\cdot S_k^{(1,2)} + S_k^{(1,2)}\cdot S_i^{(1,2)}]$  is calculated in the nth unit equilateral triangle of the lattice, and the upper corner marks 1 and 2 represent two cornershared triangles (highlighted in Figure 1c).  $\chi_{\mathrm{L},r_n^{(1,2)}} = S_i^{(1,2)}\cdot (S_j^{(1,2)}\times S_k^{(1,2)})$  is the chirality localized in the unit equilateral triangle.  $S_{i}^{(1,2)},S_{j}^{(1,2)}$  ,and  $S_{k}^{(1,2)}$  are the three spin vectors in the anticlockwise lattice. We calculate the  $Q$  and  $\chi_{\mathrm{L}}$  values in three sublattices of the triangular lattice, wherein the spin vectors run in each sublattice. Also, we calculate the total local chirality without the sublattice distinction.

Under  $h / J = 0.19$ , the spin structure of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  is roughly similar to the case under  $h / J = 0$ , displaying an AFM SS phase (see Figure 3a). From the spin texture shown in Figure 3a, no skyrmion can be observed; however,  $Q$  is calculated to be nonzero. Such a nonzero  $Q$  is correlated to the vortices formed at the intersections of different stripes as  $Q$  is characterized by the product of vortex and polarity. Meanwhile, as shown in Figure S5, with rising  $h / J$  in phase I, the number of intersections increases and thus  $Q$  increases.

For the case under  $h / J = 2.85$ , as displayed in Figure 3b, a regular array of AFM skyrmions is observed, giving rise to the intriguing AF-SkX phase. The AF-SkX is interpenetrated by the

FM skyrmion crystals of its three sublattices (see Figure 3g). For these three FM skyrmion crystals, the skyrmions are present in the same size and density, and they can be converted to each other under a translation operation. From Figure 3e, it can be seen that each AFM skyrmion in AF-SkX is formed by a FM skyrmion from one sublattice and two striped-like textures from the other two sublattices. Therefore, phase II is the topologically protected AF-SkX phase. Figure 3c,h presents the spin structure of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  under  $h / J = 3.80$ . Obviously, it exhibits a similar topologically protected spin texture: namely, the AF-SkX phase. As compared with the case under  $h / J = 2.85$ ,  $Q$  of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  under  $h / J = 3.80$  is smaller, and thus its skyrmions are present in a smaller density. Accordingly, monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  hosts two topologically protected AF-SkX phases. By increasing  $h / J$  to greater than 3.80, as shown in Figure S5, the array of AFM skyrmions in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  begins to be irregular, leading to the broken AF-SkX phase. Subsequently, the AFM skyrmions disappear and sparse stripes appear, which yields the SS phase (Figure 3d,i). Such an existence of AF-SkX in Janus monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  is consistent with the previous theories studying emergent exotic phases in the antiferromagnetic triangular lattice. $^{32,46,47}$

Here, taking  $h / J = 0.76$  and 1.70 as examples of these two regions, we display their spin structures in Figure 4c,d. It can be seen that, under  $h / J = 0.76$ , SS and AF-SkX spin patterns appear in  $\mathrm{P}+$  and  $\mathrm{P}-$ , respectively. Under the ferroelectric transition,  $\mathrm{P}+$  can be transformed into  $\mathrm{P}-$ , which is accompanied by the transition of an SS spin pattern into an AF-SkX spin pattern. However, for the case under  $h / J = 1.70$ , an AF-SkX spin pattern appears in  $\mathrm{P}+$ , which can be readily reversed to the SS phase upon switching the ferroelectric polarization. In this regard, the ferroelectrically tunable AF-SkX is realized in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ .

From Table 2 and Table S1, we find that the magnetic parameters of the As-side heterobilayer are similar to the case for the N-side, only with slight differences in exchange coupling and DMI parameters. This can be attributed to the long distance between Cr atoms and the substrate. By comparing the spin patterns shown in Figures S6-S8, it can be seen that the spin patterns of the As-side and N-side cases share roughly similar trends for the response to an external magnetic field, but only one AF-SkX phase emerges in the As-side case. This results from the relatively small antiferromagnetic coupling in As-side case.

Finally, we discuss the strain effect on the magnetic parameters as well as the topological spin structures of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . As shown in Figure S11, with increasing strain,  $J$  increases monotonically, while  $d_{xy}$  decreases monotonically, leading to the monotonic decrease of  $d_{xy} / J$ . This indicates that the topological spin structures of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  would also vary with strain. Figure S12 shows the specific spin patterns of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  under different strains and  $h / J$  values. We can see that the AF-SkX phase appears under  $h / J = 0.76$ , 1.33, and 2.66 for the cases of  $3\%$ ,  $1\%$ , and  $-1\%$  strain, respectively. However for the case of  $-3\%$  strain, the AF-SkX phase is not observed within the considered  $h / J$ . By comparing with the pure case, it is interesting to note that strain can be used to significantly reduce the required  $h / J$  to realize the AF-SkX phase. Moreover, as shown in Figure S12, along with increasing strain, the size of AFM skyrmions in AF-SkX is reduced, which is favorable for practical applications.

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, using first-principles calculations and MC simulations, we demonstrate the existence of the AF-SkX phase in the 2D lattice of monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . The skyrmion physics in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  is correlated to its large DMI and the geometric frustration of its triangular lattice. The evaluation of topological spin textures in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  with  $h / J$  and temperature is comprehensively investigated. Distinct from those reported in 3D systems, two different antiferromagnetic skyrmion phases can be manifested in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$ . Furthermore, with interfacing to monolayer  $\mathrm{Sc}_2\mathrm{CO}_2$ , the creation and annihilation of AF-SkX in monolayer  $\mathrm{CrSi}_2\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{As}_2$  are achieved by ferroelectric polarization switching. The explored phenomena and insights greatly enrich the AFM skyrmion physics in a 2D lattice.

# METHODS

DFT Method. Our first-principles calculations are performed based on density functional theory (DFT) as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP).[73,74] The projector augmented wave (PAW) method is adopted to treat the ionic potential.[75] The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) is used for the exchange-correction interactions.[76] To describe the strong correlations of 3d electrons well, the  $\mathrm{GGA + U}$  method is adopted with an effective Hubbard  $U = 3\mathrm{eV}$  for the 3d electrons of a Cr atom.[77] Taking  $U = 5.0\mathrm{eV}$  as an example, we perform test calculations to check the effect of Hubbard  $U$  value and find that the results are roughly similar to the main conclusions based on  $U = 3.0\mathrm{eV}$  (see Note 13 in the Supporting Information). The plane-wave cutoff energy is set to  $520\mathrm{eV}$ . The convergence criteria for force and energy are set to  $0.001\mathrm{eV / A}$  and  $10^{-6}\mathrm{eV}$ , respectively. The Monkhorst-Pack  $k$ -point mesh of  $13\times 13\times 1$  is adopted to sample the 2D Brillouin zone. The DFT-D3 method is employed for treating van der Waals interactions in the heterostructures. Phonon spectra are obtained by the PHONOPY code based on a  $4\times 4\times 1$  supercell.[78]

MC Simulations. Using the magnetic parameters obtained from first-principles calculations, parallel tempering MC simulations with the Metropolis algorithm are carried out to get the energy minimum spin textures.[79] The spin textures are obtained based on a  $120 \times 120 \times 1$  supercell with  $1 \times 10^{5}$  MC steps performed for each temperature (from  $660\mathrm{K}$  gradually decreasing in steps of  $5\mathrm{K}$  to the investigated low temperature).
